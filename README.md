# Data for: Open quantum sensing data for health and energy applications: A screening of availability and gaps

This repository is a data-only copy of the dataset "Data for: Open quantum sensing data for health and energy applications: A screening of availability and gaps" by Anna Lehtinen, deposited on Zenodo: https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23138448 . Every file here is byte-identical to its copy in the Zenodo record; the SHA-256 of each file is listed in `MIRROR_MANIFEST.json`. The paper is a preprint on Preprints.org; its DOI is added here once it is posted.

## About the paper

Quantum sensing roadmaps promise impact in health, energy and infrastructure applications. As these applications mature, datasets, benchmarks and data papers will depend on openly licensed measurement data that can be reused at scale. However, it is unclear how much of such data exists. In this study, we screen 86 dataset candidates: 58 from two technology lines, quantum sensing and generative AI, each crossed with five application domains (healthcare, remote and emergency care, health digital twins, hydrogen and smart grids), and 28 from later cells built around open measured anchors. Counting SQUID-based MEG as established rather than emerging technology, we found open quantum-sensing data at reusable scale in these domains for one modality only: optically pumped magnetoencephalography (OPM-MEG), with about twenty deposits from about ten providers and no harmonized release. Furthermore, we found no open application-domain deposits for nitrogen-vacancy or Rydberg sensing. Contrary to our registered expectation, the thin quantum field rejected candidates more often than the crowded generative-AI field. We release the screening table, one row per candidate with its domain cell, verdict, measured quantity and, for rejected candidates, the rejection reason, highlighting OPM-MEG harmonization as the immediate opportunity and the need for primary data partnerships in other quantum sensing modalities.
